DAWG vs. Lock In vs. Gamified Lives: Which Self-Improvement Game Fits You?

Nov 16, 2026 · 11 min read · by Kevin Castaneda

If you've gone looking for an app to help you "lock in", you've probably run into two names: DAWG and Lock In. Both promise to turn self-improvement into a video game. You get stats, an overall rating and a rank that climbs as you do the work. Between them they have about 35,000 ratings on the US App Store, which makes them far bigger than anything in the RPG habit tracker niche I usually write about.

They are also built differently from each other, and from the app I make. So this is a straight comparison of the three on the questions that decide whether you'll still be using one a month from now: what it costs, what you can try before paying, whether it tells you what to do each day, and how it treats a bad day.

A quick disclosure: one of the three, Gamified Lives, is the app I build. I've described the other two from their own listings, and where one of them is the better fit I've said so.

The short version

  • You want to be handed a strict 60-day program, with ranks and an anime hero: DAWG
  • You want many tools in one app for a low yearly price: Lock In
  • You want to try it before paying, a plan behind your goal, and a hero you design: Gamified Lives

What is a self-improvement game app?

It's a habit and goal tracker with a game drawn over it. You finish real tasks, the app gives you experience points (XP), and a number that stands for you goes up: a level, a rank, or an overall rating (OVR) built from stats such as physical, mental and discipline. It's the same idea as a life RPG, with less game. There are no dungeons or gear to manage, only you, your stats and your rank.

The appeal is that real progress is slow. A month of training might move the scale by half a pound, while a rank can move tonight. If you'd rather understand the mechanics before choosing an app, I wrote out how to gamify your life on one sheet of paper.

DAWG: a 60-day program with ranks

DAWG (full name "DAWG - Discipline & Motivation", from DWG Labs) launched in March 2025 and reached version 10.0 the day I checked. It holds a 4.8-star average across about 7,200 US ratings. Its listing is direct about who it's for: "designed for young men who want to build discipline, confidence, and winning habits."

The screenshots show the whole loop. You get a 60-day program with a short list of daily tasks (the examples shown are things like working out for an hour, waking at 7:45 and keeping social media under four hours). Finishing them raises six stats (physical, social, discipline, mental, intellect and ambition) that roll up into one overall rating. An anime-style hero levels with you, and you climb named ranks up to Ascendant and Immortal. Around that sit a community forum, group challenges and a wall of achievements. The look is dark and dramatic, and the tone is discipline.

The thing to know before you download is the price of entry. The listing says DAWG "offers a required auto-renewing subscription to access the main functionality of the app." The in-app purchases listed on the US store are a monthly plan at $19.99 and annual plans at $34.99, $59.99 and $79.99.

Pick it if you want a program that hands you the day's tasks, you like the rank-up look, and you're ready to pay up front.

Lock In: the broad toolkit

Lock In ("Lock In - Reset Your Life", from Omega Labs) launched in June 2025 and is the bigger of the two, with a 4.8-star average across about 27,800 US ratings. Its maker describes building it "because I needed it myself."

Where DAWG hands you a program, Lock In hands you tools. You create your own habits and goals, earn XP for completing them, and watch an OVR climb through a ladder of ranks. Around the tracker there's a lot: streaks, stats and habit heatmaps, daily journaling, breathing exercises, affirmations, timers for habits you're quitting (with relapse tracking), a focus mode, a community, an AI coach "for motivation and clarity," and, going by the screenshots, meal and macro tracking.

The listing ends with a plain note: "Features described above require a subscription." The prices are lower than DAWG's. The US store lists Pro Access at several prices from $17.99 to $34.99, including yearly plans at $24.99 and $29.99, and lifetime access at $59.99.

Pick it if you already know which habits you want, you'd like journaling, breathing and quit timers in the same app, and a one-time lifetime price appeals to you.

What the reviews say

Both DAWG and Lock In are well rated overall, at 4.8 stars each. The written reviews are more divided, and they're divided in the same way.

Of the 200 most recent written US reviews of DAWG (June 2 to November 14, 2026), 78 gave five stars and 80 gave one. Of the 200 most recent for Lock In (November 16, 2025 to October 28, 2026), 93 gave five stars and 74 gave one. In both sets, most of the low ratings are about one thing: having to subscribe before being able to use the app.

That's worth knowing before you download either one. Both listings do say a subscription is required, but plenty of people only find out when they reach the payment screen. If you already know you want the app, that may not matter to you. If you're unsure, it matters a lot.

Before you pay for any of them

This applies to mine as much as theirs.

  1. Read the price list first. Every App Store listing has an "In-App Purchases" row near the bottom that shows each plan and its price. Read it before you open the app, so an offer screen can't surprise you.
  2. Check the amount on Apple's confirmation sheet. The price you approve there is the price you pay, whatever the screen before it said.
  3. Ask what the app tells you to do tomorrow morning. A rank is a reward. It isn't a plan. Look for the part that turns your goal into today's tasks.
  4. Ask what a bad day costs. You will miss one. An app that makes the next day feel like starting over is an app you'll stop opening. There's a routine for that day in how to restart a habit after missing days.
  5. Know where to cancel. Subscriptions bought in an app are managed in Settings, under your name, then Subscriptions.

How to choose

  • You want someone else to set the program. DAWG's 60 days, or a plan in Gamified Lives.
  • You want the lowest long-term price. Lock In's yearly plans or its lifetime option.

  • You want a forum and group challenges with people on the same program. DAWG.

Frequently asked questions

Is the DAWG app free?

DAWG is free to download, but its App Store listing says a subscription is required to use the app's main features. On the US App Store in November 2026 the listed prices were $19.99 a month, and annual plans at $34.99, $59.99 and $79.99.

Is the Lock In app free?

Lock In is free to download, and its App Store listing notes that the features it describes require a subscription. On the US App Store in November 2026, Pro Access was listed at prices from $17.99 to $34.99, with yearly plans at $24.99 and $29.99 and lifetime access at $59.99.

Which is cheaper, DAWG or Lock In?

Going by the prices listed on the US App Store in November 2026, Lock In is cheaper: yearly plans at $24.99 and $29.99 and a $59.99 lifetime option, against DAWG's $19.99 a month and annual plans from $34.99 to $79.99. Both listings show several price points, so check the amount on Apple's confirmation sheet before you subscribe.

DAWG vs Lock In: which is better?

They suit different people. DAWG is built around a 60-day program of daily tasks, ranks and an anime hero, and says it is designed for young men. Lock In is a broader toolkit: your own habits and goals, an overall rating, journaling, breathing exercises, quit-habit timers, calorie tracking and an AI coach. Pick DAWG if you want to be handed a strict program, and Lock In if you want many tools in one place for a lower yearly price.

Is DAWG only for men?

Anyone can download it, but DAWG's own listing describes it as designed for young men who want to build discipline, confidence and winning habits. If you want a character that looks like you, Gamified Lives lets you build your own hero with any skin, hair and eye color.

Do self-improvement game apps actually work?

They can, because points, ranks and a visible character give you a reward today for work whose real payoff is weeks away. What matters over months is whether the app tells you what to do when you open it, and how it treats the day after you miss. An app that makes coming back easy keeps you longer than one that makes a slip feel like failure.
