The Best Habit Tracker Apps for 2027, Picked by What You Need

Nov 20, 2026 · 13 min read · by Kevin Castaneda

Most "best habit tracker" lists rank apps by how many features they have. That's the wrong test. A habit tracker has one job, which is to still be open on your phone in March, and the app with the longest feature list is often the first one people abandon.

So this list is sorted by what you need the app to do. I checked every price and platform below against the App Store and Google Play in November 2026. One disclosure before the picks: I make one of these apps, Gamified Lives. It's on the list, I've said plainly who should skip it, and for most of the other nine I'll tell you where they beat it.

The short version

  • If you've quit trackers because you never knew what to do each day: Gamified Lives
  • Simple, polished, pay once, Apple devices: Streaks
  • Charts, trends and every platform: Habitify
  • A whole year on one screen, cheaply: HabitKit
  • Gentle self-care on a hard day: Finch

  • A daily schedule by time of day: Productive
  • Readers of Atomic Habits: Atoms
  • Free on Android, no ads: Loop Habit Tracker

What a habit tracker can and can't do

Tracking is worth doing. A 2016 review in Psychological Bulletin pooled 138 studies with 19,951 participants and found that people who monitored their progress toward a goal were more likely to reach it. The effect was larger when the progress was physically recorded and when someone else could see it. A tracker is a convenient way to do the recording.

What the research doesn't say is that the checkbox does the work. A habit app can do up to three jobs for you:

  • Remember. Put today's habits in front of you and remind you.
  • Count. Keep the record, the streak, the chart.
  • Decide. Tell you what to do today to get to the thing you actually want, and how big the step should be.

Nearly every app on this list is good at the first two. Only a few attempt the third, and that's the one I'd look at hardest if you've already tried a tracker and stopped. In my experience people rarely quit because the app counted badly. They quit because "work out" sat on the list for a week with no plan behind it, or because one missed day turned the app into a record of failure.

For each app I went through its current store listing in November 2026 and noted four things: what it costs, where it runs, what it does besides tracking, and who it suits. 1. Gamified Lives: the plan and the checkbox

This is mine, so weigh what I say accordingly. I built it for the third job in the list above. You don't start by typing habits into an empty screen. You pick a plan (save money, read more books, build muscle, sleep on a schedule, meditate, or quit a habit), answer a few questions, and it works out the numbers and sets up the tasks, each with a line on how to do it. Today's task is the first thing you see when you open the app.

Around that is a game: you design a hero, every finished task earns XP, and there's a weekly boss that only real tasks can hit. A missed day takes nothing away. You can back a task with a photo, a timer or Apple Health if you want the proof.

Pick it if you've quit two or three trackers already and suspect the missing piece was a plan. Skip it if you want a plain checklist, you're on Android, or you want an Apple Watch or Mac app, because it's iPhone only. It's also new and small: it has six App Store ratings as I write this, where Finch has hundreds of thousands. Premium costs more than most apps here ($9.99 a month or $75.99 a year, with a 7-day trial), though the hero, tasks, weekly boss, squads and your first full plan are free.

2. Streaks: the simple one you pay for once

Streaks is the cleanest tracker on Apple devices and an Apple Design Award winner. You choose up to 24 habits, it shows them as big round buttons, and it syncs across iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, Mac and Vision Pro. It can read Apple Health, so a walk or a workout ticks itself off. It costs $5.99 once. No subscription, no account.

Pick it if you already know exactly what you want to do each day and just need it tracked well. This is the app where Streaks beats mine without a contest: it's simpler, it's on your wrist, and it's cheaper. Skip it if you're on Android, or if a broken streak is the thing that usually makes you quit. The whole app is built on consecutive days, which is motivating until the day it isn't. I wrote about that in streak anxiety.

3. Habitify: for people who love the data

Habitify has been around since 2016 and runs nearly everywhere: iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, Android and the web. Its strength is the record. You get completion rates, trends over time and per-habit charts, plus automatic tracking from health data. Pro is $8.99 a month, $49.99 a year or $119.99 for lifetime, and the free version works with limits.

Pick it if charts motivate you, or you switch between a phone and a computer all day. Skip it if you want the app to tell you what to do next. Habitify is excellent at showing what happened and leaves the planning to you. There's a longer comparison in Habitify vs. Gamified Lives.

4. HabitKit: a year on one screen

HabitKit shows each habit as a grid of tiles, one square per day, the way GitHub shows contributions. That's most of the app, and it's the point: you can see months of showing up at a glance, and the widgets put that grid on your home screen. It's made by a solo developer, runs on iOS and Android, and is one of the cheapest good options here. Pro is $1.99 a month, $11.99 a year or $29.99 for lifetime on the US App Store.

Pick it if you're a visual person and a full grid is its own reward. Skip it if you need reminders with some push behind them, or any help deciding what the habit should be.

5. Finch: the gentlest app on the list

Finch gives you a small bird to look after. You care for it by caring for yourself: check in on your mood, drink water, take a walk, write a few lines. It's by far the most popular app here, with about 777,000 App Store ratings averaging 4.9, and it's on both iOS and Android. The core loop is free. Finch Plus is listed at several prices depending on the plan, from $5.99 to $69.99.

Pick it if the goal right now is to be kinder to yourself and get through the day, or if harsher apps have made you feel worse. For that job it beats everything else here, mine included. Skip it if you're chasing a specific result with a number on it, like a savings target or a lifting program. I compared the two approaches in Finch vs. Gamified Lives.

6. Habitica: the original habit RPG

Habitica turns your tasks into a retro role-playing game. You make an avatar, earn gold and gear for finished tasks, collect pets, and join a party to take on quests with other people. Almost all of it is free, on iOS, Android and the web, with an optional subscription at $4.99 a month or $47.99 a year. Its community is the largest of any gamified tracker.

Pick it if you want depth, friends in the same game, and you like tinkering with a system. Skip it if setup tends to overwhelm you, or if losing things makes you quit: missed dailies cost health points, and in a party that can hurt your teammates too. If you want the game without that, I compared seven options in the best RPG habit trackers for 2027.

7. Fabulous: routines someone else designed

Fabulous started as a habit tracker and grew into a coaching app. Instead of a blank list, it walks you through programs: a morning routine that starts with a glass of water and adds a step at a time, with audio coaching along the way. It's on iOS and Android. Premium runs about $49.99 a year, with offers listed from $16.99 to $59.99.

Pick it if you want to be led and you like a calm, guided tone. Skip it if you already have a specific goal and want the plan built around that instead of around a program. More in Fabulous vs. Gamified Lives.

8. Productive: your day in order

Productive is a polished, long-running tracker with about 91,000 App Store ratings. It sorts habits into morning, afternoon and evening, so the app reads like a schedule, and it has challenges and programs if you want a starting point. It runs on iOS, Apple Watch and Android. The free version is limited. Premium is $10.99 a month, and the yearly plan is listed anywhere from $19.99 to $79.99 depending on the offer.

Pick it if your problem is fitting habits into a day and you like a tidy timeline. Skip it if the pricing range bothers you. Check what you're being charged before the trial ends.

9. Atoms: the Atomic Habits app

Atoms is the official app from James Clear's book. It asks you to write each habit the way the book does, as something you'll do at a set time and place to become a certain kind of person, and it adds short lessons as you go. It's on iOS, Apple Watch and Android. Pro is listed at $4.99 a month or $39.99 a year.

Pick it if you've read the book and want its method without building your own system. Skip it if you want to track a lot of habits at once. It's built around doing a few small ones well, which is the right idea, but it won't suit everyone.

10. Loop Habit Tracker: free, and it means it

Loop is an open-source Android app with no ads and no in-app purchases. It has more than five million downloads and about 64,000 Google Play ratings averaging 4.7. You get flexible schedules, a habit strength score that doesn't collapse after one missed day, home screen widgets, and it works offline. Your data stays on your phone and you can export it.

Pick it if you're on Android and want a solid tracker for nothing. For that it's the best value on this list. Skip it if you're on iPhone, or if you want sync, coaching or anything beyond tracking.

How to choose: four questions

You can settle this in about a minute.

  • Do you already know exactly what to do each day? If yes, you need a counter. Streaks on Apple devices, Loop on Android, HabitKit if you want the grid. If no, you need a plan first: Gamified Lives if you have a goal in mind, Fabulous or Atoms if you want to be taught a routine.
  • What made you quit the last one? If it was a broken streak, avoid apps built on streaks and pick one where a missed day costs nothing, like Finch or Gamified Lives. If it was boredom, you want a game: Habitica or Gamified Lives. If it was too much setup, you want Streaks or HabitKit.
  • Where do you need it? Android only: Loop, Habitica, HabitKit, Habitify, Finch. Phone and computer: Habitify or Habitica. Apple Watch: Streaks.
  • How do you feel about subscriptions? If you won't pay monthly, your options are Streaks ($5.99 once), Loop (free), or a lifetime unlock from HabitKit or Habitify.

Whichever you pick, start smaller than feels useful. One to three habits, each with a version you could do on your worst day. A new habit took 66 days on average to become automatic in a University College London study, with a range from 18 days to a projected 254, so the app you want is the one you'll still tolerate in ten weeks. And decide now what you'll do the day after you miss, because that day comes for everyone. Frequently asked questions

What is the best habit tracker app for 2027?

It depends on what you need it to do. For a simple, polished tracker on Apple devices with no subscription, Streaks. For charts and every platform, Habitify. For gentle self-care, Finch. For a free Android tracker, Loop. If you have quit trackers before because you never knew what to do each day, pick one that builds the plan as well as the checklist, which is what Gamified Lives does.

What is the best free habit tracker?

On Android, Loop Habit Tracker is completely free, with no ads and no in-app purchases. On iPhone there is no exact equivalent, but several apps have useful free tiers: Habitica's core game is free, Finch's daily self-care loop is free, and Gamified Lives includes a hero, tasks and your first full plan for free. Streaks is not free, but it is $5.99 once with no subscription.

Do habit tracker apps actually work?

Tracking helps. A 2016 review in Psychological Bulletin pooled 138 studies with 19,951 participants and found that people who monitored their progress were more likely to reach their goals, and that the effect was larger when the progress was physically recorded or shared with someone. An app is one way to record it. What an app can't do is want the goal for you.

Is there a habit tracker with no subscription?

Yes. Streaks is a one-time $5.99 purchase. Loop Habit Tracker is free on Android. HabitKit and Habitify both sell lifetime unlocks ($29.99 and $119.99 on the US App Store in November 2026) next to their subscriptions.

Which habit trackers work on both iPhone and Android?

Habitify, Habitica, Finch, Fabulous, HabitKit, Productive and Atoms all have iPhone and Android versions. Streaks is Apple only, Loop is Android only, and Gamified Lives is iPhone only for now.

How many habits should I track at once?

Fewer than you think. Start with one to three. Every extra habit is another box to miss, and a screen full of unchecked boxes is the most common reason people stop opening a tracker. Add the next habit after the first ones have survived a bad week.

What is the best habit tracker for ADHD?

No app treats ADHD, and what helps varies a lot from person to person. Features people often look for are a reward the moment a task is done, very small first steps, and no penalty for a missed day. Finch and Gamified Lives take nothing away when you miss. Habitica gives fast rewards, though missed dailies cost health points.

The one that's still open in March

Every app here can count. Pick on the two things that decide whether you keep opening it: whether it tells you what to do today, and what it does to you on the day you miss. If you already have your routine, buy Streaks or install Loop and be done in five minutes. If you've been here before and the routine is the part that keeps falling apart, choose the app that builds it with you.
