The Comeback Plan · Free

One page for the day
after a bad day.

You don’t need more motivation. You need a plan for the moment motivation is gone. This is it — three parts, ten minutes to set up, no app required.

1

Size the habit so it can’t be sabotaged

Most habits fail because they’re sized for your best day. Size yours for your worst one. The rule: if you couldn’t do it with the flu, at 11pm, after the worst day of your month — it’s too big.

Do this now

Take your habit and write two versions: the full version (30-minute workout) and the floor version (put on your shoes and do 10 squats). The floor version is the real habit. The full version is a bonus. A day where you only hit the floor still counts — fully.

Why it works: consistency is built by never letting a rep hit zero. A tiny rep keeps the identity alive; the size of the rep grows on its own.

2

Write your bad-day protocol — before the bad day

On a bad day you don’t make decisions — you follow defaults. So set the defaults now, while you’re calm. Fill in these three lines and put them where you’ll see them:

When I can feel the day going sideways, I’ll do the floor version of one habit — just one — and call the day a win.
My “still counts” list: the smallest actions that keep each habit alive (one page read, one dish washed, lights out by midnight, $1 moved to savings).
What I will NOT do: catastrophize the whole week, “start fresh Monday,” or delete the app. A bad day is a data point, not a verdict.

Why it works: pre-commitment removes the negotiation. The bad day was always coming — now it has a job to do.

3

The first 48 hours after a slip

The miss doesn’t break the habit. The 48 hours after it decide everything. Here’s the sequence:

0–1 hourSay the sentence out loud: “That was a miss, not a collapse.” No punishment, no doubling up tomorrow — compensating is how one miss becomes three.
Day 1Do the floor version — nothing more. The goal is one clean rep, not a comeback montage. This is your 2× day: the rep that matters most in the whole month.
Day 2Do it again, and tell one person you’re back — a friend, a group chat, anyone. A witnessed comeback is a kept comeback.

Why it works: research on self-compassion and habit recovery is consistent — people who forgive the slip return faster and last longer than people who punish it. Shame is not a strategy.
