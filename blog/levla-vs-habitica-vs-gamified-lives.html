Levla vs. Habitica vs. Gamified Lives: Which Habit RPG Actually Changes Your Real Life?

Nov 6, 2026 · Updated Nov 13, 2026 · 11 min read · by Kevin Castaneda

Habit RPGs had a quiet decade. Habitica owned the category for years, a few pet apps softened it, and then in 2026 a new wave showed up with real game design behind it. Levla is the one people keep asking me about, and it deserves the attention: the first ten minutes are the best onboarding I've seen in a habit app, full stop.

I build a competing app, so take that into account. But I've spent real time inside all three, and this comparison is organised around the one question that actually decides whether a habit RPG works for you: does the game change your real life, or does your real life just feed the game?

Levla: the best first session in the category

Levla doesn't open with a form. It opens with a story. You write down the thing you've been putting off, complete your first habit, get a level-up card with stat changes and a quip from your mentor, watch a goblin steal your gear, win it back in your first fight, open a loot satchel, and then it asks you to save your hero with an account. By the time the paywall appears you have already invested twenty minutes and own three items. That order is exactly right, and most habit apps get it backwards.

Two more things it nails. Every tab gets a short mentor tip the first time you open it, so you're never dropped into a dashboard you don't understand. And the Oath: a seven-day commitment your mentor co-signs and you sign with your finger. It's a small screen that does more for day-two retention than any notification.

Where it falls short is structural, not polish. Levla's economy runs on stamina. Habits generate it, fights spend it, and fights are where the loot comes from. That means the fun part of the app lives on a separate adventure screen, and your habits are the fuel you grind to get there. It works brilliantly for the first two weeks. To be fair, Levla never takes anything from you: its listing says a missed day never costs your character health or gear. The problem is quieter than that. In a slow week you earn less stamina, which means fewer fights and less loot, and the game can start to feel like something you're falling behind in. There is also no way to prove you did anything; a tapped checkbox is a tapped checkbox. And there's no coach beyond scripted lines, which is fine until the week the script doesn't fit.

If you want the most game per dollar, Levla is the pick. It is the most complete RPG on this list, and the lifetime price is fair.

Habitica: the original, and the clearest example of the punishment loop

Habitica launched as HabitRPG in 2013 and is still the category's reference point. The pixel-art avatar, the gear, the guilds, and the party quests are all still here, and the community is enormous. If you want a web version, third-party integrations, or a group of strangers to quest with, nothing else comes close.

The design decision that defines it is also the one that drives people away: a missed daily costs you HP, and in a party quest it costs your friends HP too. That is a genuine motivator for about three weeks. Then you have a bad week, your character dies, your party takes damage because of you, and opening the app starts to feel like walking into a lecture. I've written a longer piece on why Habitica becomes overwhelming and what to look for instead, and the short version is: the loss framing is the feature, and the feature is why the week-four drop-off is so steep.

Gamified Lives: the boss is your real life

I'll describe what we built and then be honest about where the other two are better.

The hero is yours in the literal sense: every player starts from the same blank base, and you choose hair, eyes, brows, and any skin, hair or eye colour. No photo, nothing uploaded. From there, everything the hero wears, and the room it stands in, is bought with coins that only come from completed tasks.

The boss is the part I think matters most. Each week you pick one of six bosses, one per area of life: the Couch Colossus for health, the Impulse Imp for money, the Snooze Dragon for sleep, and so on. Every real task you finish is a hit. Tasks in the boss's own area hit twice as hard. A task you proved with a photo, a timer, or Apple Health crits. There's no stamina, no separate fight screen, and nothing to grind. If you skip a day the boss heals a little, which is the only consequence; nothing is ever taken from you. Beat it and you get coins and a rare piece of gear. Your to-do list is the fight.

Underneath that is the thing the app was actually built for: the comeback. Your home screen shows Time Played, a number that only goes up, instead of a streak that can hit zero. Miss a day, lose nothing. Come back within 24 hours and the Phoenix Bonus pays double XP. That's the opposite of Habitica's loop, and it's why I don't think streaks belong in a habit app at all.

The first session borrows the best of Levla and I'll say so plainly. You get a real win in the first few minutes, a guide card the first time you open each screen, a starter quest that walks you through the five tabs, a "watch your hero grow" preview, and a seven-day Oath you sign with your finger. Keep the Oath and you earn a title for it. Then there's the coach: an AI that knows your goals and habits, writes you a weekly report across every life area, and that you can call for a real voice conversation.

Where it loses. It is iOS only. The community is a fraction of Habitica's. If you want a world to explore, with vendors, a map, and a stamina economy to optimise, Levla is simply more game, and ours will feel sparse by comparison. And the coach and the rare gear are Premium, which costs more per month than Levla.

How to choose in three questions

  1. What happens on your first bad week? If you've quit habit apps before, this is the only question. Habitica punishes it, Levla slows down with you, Gamified Lives rewards the return. Pick the one whose answer you can live with on a Thursday in week four.
  2. Do you want the game to be your life, or a reward for it? Levla is a game you feed with habits. Gamified Lives is a life tracker with the game drawn on top of it. Habitica sits in between. Neither extreme is wrong; they attract different people.
  3. Does a tapped checkbox count? If you know you'll tap "done" on things you didn't do, you need proof-based completion, and only one of the three has it.

Frequently asked questions

Is Levla free?

Levla has a free tier. At the time of writing, Premium is $5.99 a month, $49.99 a year, or $99.99 for lifetime access, with a 7-day trial on the yearly plan. Gamified Lives is free on iOS with Premium at $9.99 a month or $75.99 a year with a 7-day trial. Habitica is free with an optional subscription for gems and extras.

Levla vs Habitica: which is better?

Levla is the more modern game: a story-driven first session, mentor tips on every tab, an oath you sign, and a full gear and stamina economy. Habitica is older but has a huge community, parties, and the deepest third-party ecosystem. They treat a missed day differently: Habitica takes HP, while Levla takes nothing away, though a slow week earns less stamina and so fewer adventures. Neither one rewards the comeback itself.

Is there a habit RPG with boss fights tied to real tasks?

Yes. In Gamified Lives you pick a boss each week for the area of life you are pushing. Every real task you complete hits it, tasks in that area hit harder, photo- or sensor-verified tasks crit, and a missed day only lets the boss heal a little. There is no stamina to spend and no fight screen separate from your actual to-do list.

Does Gamified Lives need a photo of your face?

No. Every hero starts from the same blank base, and you pick hair, eyes, brows, and any skin, hair or eye color in the Avatar Builder. Nothing about your face is collected.

Which habit RPG is best for ADHD?

The one that survives a bad week. Look for a forgiving miss rule, a first session that gets you a win within minutes, and proof-based completion so the dopamine is attached to real work. Gamified Lives was built around those three; Levla's playable onboarding is excellent for getting started and a missed day costs nothing there, but a slow week earns less stamina, so you get less of the game.

All three apps understand something most productivity software doesn't: people will do an astonishing amount of work for a progress bar. The difference is what the bar is attached to. Levla attaches it to a game. Habitica attaches it to not losing. We tried to attach it to the thing you were trying to change in the first place.
